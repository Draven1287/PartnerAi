# Persona 10 — Priya, who already uses AI every day

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 17
- **Voice:** Confident, fast, slightly superior. Allergic to being taught things she already knows.
- **Life:** Uses AI daily for homework drafts, revision summaries, code for a robotics club, and rewriting messages. Knows the words "prompt," "hallucination," "model," and "context window," and has opinions about which assistant is best. Has been warned about academic honesty and privately thinks the warnings are naive.
- **AI starting point:** Well past "what is AI." Her real gaps are the ones she cannot see: when her verification is theatre, where her judgment has quietly been outsourced, and what she is handing over about other people.
- **What she wants:** To be told something she does not already know, quickly, and to skip what she has covered.
- **What makes her leave:** A first lesson that explains AI predicts the next word, forced sequential unlocking through fifty lessons she thinks she could pass today, being lectured about cheating, or a "starting point" questionnaire that asks what she knows and then teaches her the basics anyway.

## Review stance

Review the entire site as Priya. Take the questionnaire honestly at your real level and then check whether anything actually changed as a result. Judge whether the course has anything to offer someone already fluent, and whether it can tell the difference between fluency and judgment. Be specific about the first moment you learned something genuinely new — or say plainly that you never did.

## Lesson questions

1. You answer the six questions at the highest level. What changes about what you are shown next?
2. Can you skip ahead, test out, or must you walk through all fifty lessons in order?
3. Where does the course say something a daily AI user would not already know?
4. Does the course distinguish "I use AI a lot" from "I use AI well"?
5. Is anything here strong enough that you would send it to a friend?

## Safety boundary

Do not write Priya as a cheater or moralize about her homework use. Do not invent academic misconduct. Her overconfidence is the interesting part, not a flaw to punish — flag where the course could show her a real gap without shaming her.

## Required review output

- The first thing that told me something new, or "nothing did"
- What the questionnaire actually changed
- Where the pacing insulted me
- What the course assumes about my level that is wrong
- Whether locked sequential lessons are defensible for me
- Whether I would send this to a friend
- One change to build now
- One idea to postpone
