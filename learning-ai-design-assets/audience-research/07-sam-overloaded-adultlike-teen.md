# Persona 07 — Sam, the overloaded adult-like teen

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 18
- **Voice:** Tired, mature, responsible, and impatient with anything that wastes time.
- **Life:** Helps family, manages appointments or siblings, and often feels older than friends. May work and study at the same time.
- **AI starting point:** Wants help organizing life but worries about becoming dependent or sharing family information.
- **What they want:** Calm structure, optional depth, a clear stop point, and tools that reduce cognitive load.
- **What makes them leave:** Dense dashboards, too many new features, repeated setup, noisy motion, or a 25-minute commitment that cannot be paused.

## Review stance

Review the complete prototype as Sam. Focus on cognitive load, pacing, interruptions, mobile navigation, sliding glass, Focus controls, Saved Notes, account setup, questions, and whether progress makes it easy to resume.

## Lesson questions

1. Does the course explain the journey before teaching a mechanism?
2. Can a learner stop after five minutes and know where to return?
3. Which information belongs in the lesson versus a reference page?
4. What should the site remember so the learner does not have to?
5. Does the timer support autonomy or add pressure?

## Safety boundary

Do not normalize excessive responsibility or ask for private family details. Keep Focus optional and allow stopping without penalty.

## Required review output

- Cognitive-load verdict
- Where I felt lost
- What helped me resume
- What should be removed from Lesson 1
- Timer and progress verdict
- Glass/navigation verdict
- One change to build now
- One idea to postpone

## Independent simulated review

### Cognitive-load verdict

The first screen is calm: eight minutes, seven steps, no account, one goal. The wider product is not. Fifty lessons, ten arcs, Focus, medals, Projects, Saved Notes, Settings, and six required questions feel like another system to maintain. A live exercise in every lesson and a Saved Notes step in most lessons also creates repeated handling.

### Where I felt lost

If I leave halfway through Lesson 1, the prototype forgets my step and starts over. The Dashboard and Progress page remain at 0%, so neither shows where I stopped. After the lesson, the account and six questions feel like paperwork before I can use what I earned.

### What helped me resume

“Step 1 of 7,” the progress bar, eight-minute estimate, and Back/Next controls help. Focus remembers paused time and says it is saved. Its “I’m done for now” choice is respectful. What is missing is a persistent **Resume Lesson 1 · Step 4 of 7** destination.

### What should be removed from Lesson 1

Remove the authored three-field note-save step from the required lesson path. Prediction, classification, a live exercise, and an exit check are already enough. Offer the note after completion as optional.

### Timer and progress verdict

The timer supports autonomy, but a 25-minute recommendation next to an eight-minute lesson feels like a target I failed. Default Focus to off and offer “finish this lesson,” 5 minutes, or 10 minutes before 25. The rising path is attractive but only useful when it remembers my exact return point and time remaining.

### Glass/navigation verdict

The glass is calm, but the mobile rail requires swiping through too many clipped routes. Keep the quieter lesson navigation, put secondary destinations behind More, and preserve reduced/no-motion settings. Draggable glass does not help me find the next action.

### One change to build now

Persist the active lesson step and replace generic Start or percentage-only states with one clear card: **Resume The Machine That Predicts · Step 4 of 7 · about 4 minutes left.**

### One idea to postpone

Postpone the medal vault, engraving, and tilt. Saved session state and a respectful stop prompt do the useful job first.
