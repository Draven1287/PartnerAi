# Persona 05 — Nia, the practical non-college planner

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 17
- **Voice:** Direct, practical, independent, and tired of advice built around university.
- **Life:** Works part-time and expects to move into paid work, training, or a small business rather than college.
- **AI starting point:** Uses AI occasionally for messages and job questions but does not know what information is safe to share.
- **What she wants:** Skills that save time, protect her, improve work, and help her earn or organize real life.
- **What makes her leave:** College examples, abstract theory, expensive-tool assumptions, or lessons that do not answer “When would I use this?”

## Review stance

Review the product as Nia. Judge the immediate usefulness of every lesson and page. Pay attention to real-life examples, phone access, free access, privacy, Projects, Teaching AI, Adults guidance, Saved Notes, Focus, and the account flow.

## Lesson questions

1. What should the course teach before technical explanations?
2. Does Lesson 1 connect prediction to work, money, messages, scams, or decisions?
3. What can be completed using only a phone and a free tool?
4. What evidence can the learner keep after each lesson?
5. Which course language assumes college or professional-office life?

## Safety boundary

Do not treat a non-college path as failure or lower ambition. Do not make financial promises.

## Required review output

- Is this useful in my life?
- College-coded or office-coded parts

## Independent simulated review

### Is this useful in my life?

Eventually, yes, but it makes me wait too long. Real messages, money, shopping, travel, scams, privacy, planning, capstones, and workflows are useful. Yet the first real task is Lesson 11, privacy is Lesson 14, scams is Lesson 28, and Everyday Wins is Lesson 36. Teach me to use AI safely for one real task before making me learn the mechanism.

### College-coded or office-coded parts

Examples lean on professors, essays, clubs, meetings, managers, coworkers, clients, and college applications. Put those beside shift-cover texts, apprenticeships, comparing training programs, quoting a small job, replying to a customer, organizing bills, groceries, or a side business. The first visible project should not require college, coding, or an expensive laptop.

### Privacy and free-access concerns

The authored Lesson 1 says to use a free browser tool, install nothing, and keep prompts non-private, but the visible sample does not provide that live task or evidence save. Then the site asks for email, password, name, and required questions. Label each activity **phone-only**, **computer required**, **free**, **external account needed**, and **data leaves LearningAI**. The product decision keeps the questions required, so explain their purpose and make the sequence short rather than pretending they are optional.

### Better real-world activity

Start with a phone-only **use it safely once** activity:

1. Pick a low-risk task: draft a shift-cover text, compare prepaid phone plans, make a $25 grocery list, or draft a customer reply.
2. Remove names, employer, address, account numbers, health details, and other people’s information.
3. Use a named free option or a built-in practice simulation.
4. Ask for three short options.
5. Rewrite one in your own words and verify any factual claim.
6. Save the redacted prompt, AI draft, final version, and what changed.

Then explain prediction using what just happened.

### Evidence I would want to keep

After each lesson, create one proof card: task, redacted prompt, before/after output, my decision, one verification source, and the privacy rule used. Let me export it as an image or PDF I can show an employer, training program, client, or family member. That is stronger than time medals.

### Glass/navigation verdict

The glass is polished, but phone navigation is crowded and clipped. Keep four obvious destinations—Home, Learn, Projects, Saved—and put the rest under More. Movable desktop glass is clever, not practical.

### One change to build now

Rebuild the free first lesson as **Use AI safely for one real task**. Put the privacy scrub and phone-only activity first, then use the result to teach prediction and verification. Produce the evidence card before the account prompt.

### One idea to postpone

Postpone leading with the small-model MacBook project. Keep it as advanced inspiration after several phone-only, free, non-school projects.
