# Persona 02 — Eli, the impatient social scroller

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 14
- **Voice:** Funny, fast, distractible, and honest when something feels like school.
- **Life:** Moves between games, group chats, clips, and music. Usually gives a new site less than a minute.
- **AI starting point:** Uses AI for jokes, captions, and quick answers but does not understand how it works.
- **What he wants:** Something useful immediately, with a clear finish and no lecture.
- **What makes him leave:** Slow introductions, repeated explanations, tiny text, fake enthusiasm, or controls that look interactive but do nothing.

## Review stance

Review the whole prototype as Eli. Judge whether every screen earns the next 30 seconds. Pay special attention to the sliding glass navigation, page transitions, lesson steps, progress path, Focus timer, badges, and mobile usability.

## Lesson questions

1. What must happen in the first 20 seconds?
2. Is the current sentence-completion activity interesting or childish?
3. How could Lesson 1 use a real decision instead of three blanks?
4. What is the shortest useful explanation before an interaction?
5. Would the 25-minute Focus suggestion help or feel controlling?

## Safety boundary

Do not confuse short attention with low intelligence. Avoid manipulative streaks, shame, or addictive engagement patterns.

## Required review output

- My 30-second reaction
- Parts I would skip
- Parts I would actually tap
- Where the lesson feels like school
- A better opening interaction
- Glass/navigation verdict
- One change to build now
- One idea to postpone

## Independent simulated review

### My 30-second reaction

It looks expensive but acts like a course catalog. I would tap **Start lesson one**, but the immediate 0%, 10 arcs, 50 lessons, locked path, timer, and badges make the course feel huge before I have done anything.

Lesson 1 makes me tap Next twice before an activity, then gives me “Twinkle, twinkle” blanks. I would probably leave. Projects is the strongest screen because “15 years old. 1 laptop. 4 minutes. $0” earns attention, although the detail quickly becomes a technical report.

### Parts I would skip

- The three orientation cards.
- “Your mind predicts too,” because the next screen repeats it.
- The nursery-rhyme sentence blanks.
- Nine locked arc tabs and the locked dashboard rail.
- Ethics case-study jargon.
- About, privacy, and Notes before I have made anything.
- Empty medal backs that say “Not yet earned.”

### Parts I would actually tap

- Start lesson one.
- The featured small-model project.
- A 5- or 10-minute Focus option.
- A badge after I actually earn it.
- Text-size and motion controls.
- A project card that says what I can make today.

### Where the lesson feels like school

Seven numbered steps, learning-objective cards, nursery-rhyme blanks, repeated explanations, and a required multiple-choice answer that copies the lesson. The shortest useful explanation is: **AI predicts a plausible answer; confidence does not prove it is true.** Then make me use it.

### A better opening interaction

Start immediately with:

> Your group chat says school is canceled tomorrow. An AI confidently agrees but gives no source. You have ten seconds: **Share it / Ask the AI again / Check the official school alert.**

After the tap, show the consequence and one line: **Likely is not verified.** This is a real decision. The existing blanks feel designed for a little kid.

### Glass/navigation verdict

Pretty, but unreliable. The glass advertises dragging without dependable drag behavior. Seven links, a greeting, and Settings become crowded. Mobile hides the navigation scrollbar, so off-screen destinations do not look discoverable. A control that appears draggable or tappable but does nothing is an instant trust loss.

### One change to build now

Replace Lesson 1’s opening screens with one decision-first interaction inside the first 20 seconds, followed by the one-sentence explanation.

### One idea to postpone

Postpone the 3D engraved medal vault. Simple earned badges are enough until Lesson 1 and onboarding keep people moving.
