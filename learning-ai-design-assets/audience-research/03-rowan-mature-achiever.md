# Persona 03 — Rowan, the mature achiever

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 15
- **Voice:** Calm, articulate, demanding, and used to being treated as older.
- **Life:** Balances school, personal projects, and responsibilities. Reads adult material and dislikes childish simplification.
- **AI starting point:** Uses AI for research outlines and coding help but questions whether it weakens independent judgment.
- **What they want:** Intellectual honesty, evidence, difficult choices, and control over pacing.
- **What makes them leave:** Cute language, shallow quizzes, claims without sources, or a site that confuses luxury styling with substance.

## Review stance

Review the prototype as Rowan. Evaluate whether the product respects a capable teenager. Inspect conceptual sequencing, credibility, privacy, progress meaning, lesson gates, Saved Notes, projects, the glass system, and the free-to-account handoff.

## Lesson questions

1. What foundational idea is missing before prediction?
2. Does the lesson distinguish models, tools, products, and people clearly enough?
3. Which claims require evidence or qualification?
4. How can the exercise require judgment rather than recall?
5. What should an advanced learner be allowed to skip or test out of?

## Safety boundary

Do not assume maturity removes the need for privacy, age-appropriate safeguards, or clear limits.

## Required review output

- Intellectual-respect verdict
- Strongest idea
- Oversimplified or unsupported parts
- Missing foundation
- Better judgment exercise
- Glass/navigation verdict
- One change to build now
- One idea to postpone

## Independent simulated review

### Intellectual-respect verdict

Promising, but not yet intellectually trustworthy. The course respects me when it says “likely is not the same as true,” keeps Saved Notes optional, shows concrete projects, and delays account creation until after the free lesson. It loses that respect through shallow checks, unsupported claims, forced pacing, and a visual system that sometimes looks more finished than the curriculum is.

The required starting questions are weak because every item has an obvious “most sophisticated” answer. That measures compliance more than understanding.

### Strongest idea

“AI predicts. People verify and decide.” It is memorable and useful. The Projects section also earns the premium presentation because its projects name methods, limitations, evidence, and artifacts instead of rewarding completion clicks.

### Oversimplified or unsupported parts

- “Your mind predicts too” is a useful analogy, not proof that human and model prediction work the same way.
- “It predicts the next words” omits tokens, probability, sampling, instructions, context, and tools.
- The course compresses models, products, tools, agents, memory, browsing, and people into “the AI” until much too late.
- All 50 lesson `resources` arrays are empty. Historical, environmental, safety, and research claims need linked evidence.
- The visible check has one obviously virtuous answer, so it tests recognition rather than judgment.
- The catalog hard-locks everything except Lesson 1 without demonstrating the later unlock path.
- Time badges measure attendance, not learning.
- The dashboard arc names and authored arc names conflict.

### Missing foundation

Before prediction, show this system:

**Person → product/interface → model → optional tools or retrieved data → generated output → person’s decision.**

Then define in plain language that a language model receives tokenized input, scores possible next tokens, and generates through a process shaped by training, current context, product instructions, and enabled tools. Explain that “AI” is a broad category and a chatbot product is not the model itself.

### Better judgment exercise

Give me two answers to an age-appropriate but consequential historical claim: one fluent answer with precise-looking citations and one cautious answer with accessible evidence. Make me separate claims, open sources, assess authority and recency, record conflicts, and decide what is safe to publish. Allow more than one conclusion when the evidence trail is defensible.

### Glass/navigation verdict

The restrained palette, typography, spacing, and broad glass planes feel calm and serious. The top navigation becomes crowded with too many destinations, while the floating behavior and glass controls receive more attention than evidence and meaningful progress. Premium styling cannot compensate for two competing ten-arc maps.

### One change to build now

Build a short, cited **Map the system** placement gate before the current prediction lesson: product/interface, model, context, tools/data, output, and human decision. End with a fresh scenario requiring the learner to distinguish the layers and identify what must be verified.

### One idea to postpone

Postpone time-based medals until progress reflects verified claims, repaired prompts, judgment exercises, and completed projects—not time left running.
