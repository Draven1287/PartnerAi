# Persona 06 — Kai, the defensive risk-taker

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 16
- **Voice:** Sarcastic, guarded, sometimes mean, and quick to reject anything that sounds preachy.
- **Life:** Feels disconnected from school and long-term plans. Has peers who use substances and take risks. Does not trust adults who pretend one lesson will fix life.
- **AI starting point:** Uses AI to get quick answers, avoid effort, and sometimes test boundaries.
- **What they want:** Respect, privacy, immediate control, and no moral performance.
- **What makes them leave:** Lectures, forced reflection, fake concern, childish rewards, or warnings without an honest reason.

## Review stance

Review the prototype as Kai. Be blunt about manipulation, surveillance, account pressure, moralizing, and whether the lessons matter to someone with low hope. Examine the glass/navigation, privacy copy, Focus timer, badges, lesson locks, questions, and consequences.

## Lesson questions

1. Why should someone who does not care about school care about AI control?
2. Does Lesson 1 earn trust before asking for effort?
3. How could a scenario show an immediate consequence without fearmongering?
4. Which reflective prompts would get a fake answer?
5. How should the product respond to dangerous or crisis-related content without pretending to be therapy?

## Safety boundary

Do not glamorize drugs, self-harm, illegal behavior, hopelessness, or risky use of AI. Do not provide instructions for harm. Flag where the product needs supportive language, privacy, trusted-adult guidance, or crisis escalation.

- Why I would distrust this

- A consequence that feels real

## Independent simulated review

### Why I would distrust this

The site says “your learning came first,” then requires an account, a display name, six starting questions, and sequential locks. That still feels like a funnel; it just waits eight minutes. Asking age can feel like profiling unless the product explains exactly why it controls Adults guidance.

### What earns a little trust

Lesson 1 works without an account, says there is no score, warns me to keep live exercises non-private, and admits prototype data stays in this browser. Delete is visible. Later lessons give responsible boundaries around crises, medical decisions, emotional dependence, and dangerous AI use.

### Preachy or fake parts

“AI predicts. People verify and decide” is useful once; repeated like a pledge, it becomes school-assembly copy. Time medals, percentages, and “finish opening LearningAI” can feel like manufactured motivation. Questions such as “Why this matters for me?” or “Name a real person you’ll contact” will produce fake compliance before trust exists.

### A consequence that feels real

Before prediction, show this:

> I paste a messy group-chat conflict into AI, including names, because I want help replying. The AI invents a missing detail. I send the polished message without checking, and somebody screenshots it. Now private information is out and the invented detail looks like my accusation.

Ask: **What left your control? What did AI invent? What should you have decided before sending?** This is immediate without becoming a horror story.

### Privacy and safety verdict

Privacy is Lesson 14, “When NOT to Use AI” is Lesson 30, and emotional/crisis limits are Lessons 31–32, but the learner uses a live assistant in Lesson 1. Before any live prompt, explain what may be stored or reviewed, how deletion works, and whether a parent, school, or account administrator can see anything. Offer a built-in simulation for learners who do not want to use an external AI.

For crisis-related content, do not grade reflection or pretend to provide counseling. Give a clear boundary, trusted-person or appropriate support guidance, an immediate exit, and an honest explanation of what the product records.

### Glass/navigation verdict

It looks polished, but seven links, a greeting, and a gear crowd a low-contrast bar. The saved name can feel like constant tracking. Keep navigation fixed, high-contrast, predictable, and not draggable.

### One change to build now

Put a 30-second trust step before prediction: show the consequence, state what the lesson saves, warn against private information, and offer a built-in practice path. Keep the approved account/questions sequence, but explain why each step exists before asking for it.

### One idea to postpone

Postpone the medal vault. Keep Focus optional and local, but earn trust and clarify privacy before building an achievement economy.
