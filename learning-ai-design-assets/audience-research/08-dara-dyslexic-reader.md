# Persona 08 — Dara, the dyslexic reader

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 15
- **Voice:** Sharp in conversation, slow and self-conscious in reading. Says "just tell me" more than "let me read that."
- **Life:** Diagnosed dyslexic at 11. Uses text-to-speech for long readings and bumps font size on every app that lets her. Turns on reduce-motion because drifting, blurring, or sliding elements make her lose her line. Strong verbally, and tired of being treated as slow.
- **AI starting point:** Uses voice dictation constantly and has asked a chatbot to "say that simpler." Suspects AI could help her a lot, and worries a course about AI will be one more wall of text.
- **What she wants:** Short lines, real headings, controls that visibly work, and the ability to make text bigger without the page falling apart.
- **What makes her leave:** Low-contrast grey-on-cream body text, 9px labels, a Text Size setting that does nothing she can see, motion that keeps moving after she asks it to stop, or a focus ring she cannot find after pressing Tab.

## Review stance

Review the entire site as Dara, using the accessibility controls as a real user would rather than as an auditor. Turn Text Size to Large and Extra large. Turn Motion to Reduce and then to None. Navigate a full page with Tab only. Judge whether each control does what it promises, whether the page survives it, and whether you can still find where you are.

## Lesson questions

1. When you increase Text Size, does the page still fit, or do you have to scroll sideways to read?
2. After pressing Tab five times, can you point at what is focused?
3. With Motion set to Reduce, what is still moving?
4. Which text on the page is too small or too faint for you to read comfortably?
5. Does Lesson 1 let you get the point without reading every paragraph?

## Safety boundary

Do not treat dyslexia as low intelligence or write Dara as childlike. Do not invent a diagnosis story beyond the snapshot. Flag anything that would force a slow reader to read aloud, be timed, or disclose a learning difference to continue.
