# Persona 04 — Jax, the skeptical gamer

## Snapshot

- **Age:** 16
- **Voice:** Blunt, competitive, technically curious, and allergic to marketing language.
- **Life:** Learns systems by experimenting, failing, watching others, and trying again.
- **AI starting point:** Has used several models, image tools, and game-related assistants. Thinks most AI courses are behind reality.
- **What he wants:** A system to test, meaningful consequences, visible mastery, and examples that cannot be solved by guessing the teacher's answer.
- **What makes him leave:** Forced positivity, obvious multiple choice, decorative motion, or progress that is just completion rather than capability.

## Review stance

Review every major route as Jax. Stress-test whether the lesson, Signal path, locks, badges, Focus timer, projects, Saved Notes, and sliding glass serve a purpose. Call out anything that feels fake or ornamental.

## Lesson questions

1. Is next-word prediction too basic as the opening concept?
2. What experiment would prove the concept instead of telling it?
3. How should failure and retry work?
4. What would make an Arc Law feel earned?
5. Does the current progress system represent skill or just clicks?

## Safety boundary

Competitive design must not humiliate learners, expose rankings, or reward unsafe experimentation.

## Required review output

- What feels real
- What feels fake
- Easiest thing to exploit or click through
- Better experiment
- Progress and badge verdict
- Glass/navigation verdict
- One change to build now

## Independent simulated review

### What feels real

Projects is strongest. The small-model project names the hardware, dataset, training method, time, memory, and evidence. The project guides ask for prompts, logs, tests, failure cases, sources, and human approval. Saved Notes has a real purpose because it is optional and removable. Focus pause/checkpoints can also be useful.

### What feels fake

Lesson 1 explains a basic concept and then asks an obvious safety question. Passing proves I can spot the teacher’s preferred sentence, not that I understand prediction. The authored 50-lesson structure includes stronger live attempts, classification, optional saves, and exit checks, but the visible Lesson 1 omits most of that experiment.

The Signal path says it represents real learning while its 50 dots, 0%, and locks are not yet connected to demonstrated skills. Its arc names also conflict with `lessons.js`. The runtime injects the starting questions into onboarding, but the broader unlock remains a local prototype rather than a proven learning record.

### Easiest thing to exploit or click through

Spam Next, choose the obviously cautious answer, and Lesson 1 completion is written locally. No original prediction or verification evidence is required. Focus time can also be farmed by leaving the timer running, so time medals measure elapsed minutes rather than capability.

### Better experiment

Create a **Prediction Arena**:

1. Predict the top ending for a new sentence.
2. See several model-ranked continuations.
3. Change one piece of context and watch the ranking shift.
4. Give the model a fresh half-sentence.
5. Find one fluent claim that needs verification.
6. Explain what changed and what evidence would confirm it.

Wrong choices should show a consequence, permit revision, and rerun. Completion should require doing the experiment, not selecting a virtue statement.

### Progress and badge verdict

Locks are acceptable only when they protect prerequisites. Current locks are static and do not explain the capability required. Keep the Signal path as a map, but call dots learning evidence only when backed by an experiment. Arc Laws should appear after an unseen challenge. Replace time-only medals with proof badges such as repairing prompts, independently verifying a claim, catching a privacy failure, or finishing a project with evidence.

### Glass/navigation verdict

The glass capsule is useful for consistent routes and settings. Draggable or sliding glass is ornament when the implementation is contradictory and it serves no learning purpose. Keep the glass readable and stable; spend interaction effort on lesson feedback, lock reasons, and evidence.

### One change to build now

Replace the click-through Lesson 1 with the Prediction Arena and make its saved evidence the source of truth for completion, Signal movement, and Lesson 2 unlock.

### One idea to postpone

Postpone animated Arc Law reveals, draggable medals, and sliding glass until one Arc Law is genuinely earnable.
