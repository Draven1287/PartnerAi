Teaching AI

Teach capability and judgment together. Begin with a real choice, let the learner try, reveal what the system is doing, and finish with a decision the learner can explain without AI.

  • Model the boundary: use invented information and name what should stay private.
  • Ask before explaining: let the learner predict, choose, or test before giving the answer.
  • Check outside the model: use independent evidence whenever an answer affects a real person or decision.
  • Return control: the learner edits, rejects, or approves the final action and explains why.

The adult-only workflow guidance appears separately after an adult age range is saved. This section is the teaching method used throughout the course.

Who this is for

Teenagers who are curious, worried, already using AI for school, or not sure where to begin. No coding background is required. Adults can use the course, but should expect teen-first examples and pacing.

What you will learn

How AI predicts, where it can mislead you, how to check important claims, how to protect private information, and how to build useful workflows that always return the final decision to you.

Who made it

LearningAI is directed by Aarav Shah and developed with teachers, learners, and AI collaborators. The educational choices, boundaries, and final decisions remain human-authored and human-reviewed.

Why it exists

AI is becoming part of school, work, and everyday decisions. The goal is not to make the technology feel magical. It is to help people question, verify, direct, and stop it when necessary.

How it came about

The course grew from one practical question: how can people gain capability with AI while keeping control? Ten arcs build from first contact to durable, human-approved workflows.

For adults

Parents, teachers, and adult learners can follow the same course. Adult-only guidance explains how to support a teenager without taking over, and how to turn a repeated task into a human-approved workflow.

Privacy commitment

  • No advertising and no selling learner information.
  • Accounts store identity, lesson progress, and starting-question answers only to provide the learning experience.
  • Learners can change their display name and delete their account and learning data in Settings.
  • Do not enter sensitive health, financial, school-record, or identifying information into lesson exercises.
  • Adults should support younger learners when a project involves another person’s information.

Account creation, Lesson 1 progress, and starting-question answers use the LearningAI backend. Deleting your account removes your answers, progress, and saved notes from LearningAI.
