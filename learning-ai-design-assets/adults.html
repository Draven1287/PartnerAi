Six steps to practise together

One pass through these takes a single sitting. They work for homework, a club project, or a household task — the point is that the learner keeps the decision at the end.

01

Choose one real task

Start with a low-stakes task the learner already understands: planning, outlining, comparing options, or explaining a concept.

02

Name the human decision

Before opening AI, decide what the learner must still choose, check, rewrite, or refuse. That responsibility cannot disappear into the tool.

03

Set information boundaries

Use invented examples. Do not paste names, school records, private messages, health details, passwords, or another person's information.

04

Use AI for material

Ask for options, questions, a draft, or a counterargument. Do not let a polished answer become automatic permission to act.

05

Check outside the model

Verify consequential claims with current, independent sources and ask what evidence would change the conclusion.

06

Reflect and improve

Save one useful rule: what AI helped with, what the learner changed, and what they would do differently next time.

Working with workflows

A workflow here means one thing: a request the learner writes once as numbered steps, with a single slot for the part that changes, so it can be reused without retyping. The course teaches it directly — Lesson 44, “Write It Once, Reuse It Forever” — and the two arcs around it build up to and past it.

The part worth supervising is the part the lesson is blunt about: reuse repeats mistakes as reliably as it repeats good steps. A careless habit with someone else’s information, or a missing check, now happens on every single run. So a saved workflow needs two things written into it. Nothing real about another person goes in. And a human reads the result and says yes before anything is sent, posted, booked, deleted, bought, or used to judge a person.
