30-lesson direction

Six arcs. One learning journey.

1. Orientation

Agency first, then an early useful AI conversation, then the mental models that make the tool less mysterious.

  • Why AI matters now
  • Your first useful AI conversation
  • What AI actually is
  • What an LLM is
  • Prompt repair
2. Understanding

Training, data, context, memory, hallucinations, models, and tools.

  • Data, training, and patterns
  • Context windows and memory
  • Why AI hallucinates
  • Models and tools
3. Conversation

Prompt repair, follow-ups, roles, constraints, and using AI as a tutor.

  • Prompting as clear communication
  • Better follow-ups
  • Roles, formats, and constraints
  • Getting AI to teach you
4. Judgment

Agency, verification, bias, privacy, boundaries, and when not to use AI.

  • Human agency with AI
  • Verification and sources
  • Bias and perspective
  • Privacy and personal data
  • When not to use AI
5. Application Studios

Track-specific work for students, teachers, builders, business workflows, and creative/personal use.

  • Student study studio
  • Writing and research studio
  • Coding and debugging studio
  • Creative and workflow studios
6. Building

Turn prompts into workflows, design tools, test outputs, and finish a capstone project.

  • From prompt to workflow
  • Designing AI tools
  • Evaluation and testing
  • Capstone project
Sample V2 activity

Bad tutor vs. good tutor.

The first V2 slice is planned around a Student Study Coach. The goal is simple: use AI to learn more, not think less.

Weak AI help

It gives the answer away.

"Here is the explanation. Memorize these terms and you should be fine."

Fast, but it does the thinking for you.

Better AI help

It protects your learning.

"First, try explaining the idea in one sentence. Then I will ask one hint question and check your answer before giving the full explanation."

Slower, but it helps you build the skill.

Study Coach Card

V2 lessons will help you build cards like this and save them into your AI Toolkit.

  • Topic: a class, project, work task, or personal goal
  • Goal: understand the idea well enough to use it
  • Rule: ask for hints before answers
  • Check: quiz me after I try
  • Boundary: do not replace the thinking I need to practice
