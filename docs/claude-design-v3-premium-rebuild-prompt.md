## The problem

The current V3 is structurally complete but visually unacceptable. It feels like a generic dark SaaS dashboard: flat black panels, weak grey text, small coral rules, rigid boxes, and almost no imagery, atmosphere, transformation, or delight. It does not feel premium, memorable, emotionally reassuring, or worth completing for 50 lessons. V2 felt warmer and more considered, although it is not the final visual answer either.

Do not polish the current composition. Establish a new design thesis and spatial grammar before drawing screens.

## Product purpose

Learning AI is a free 50-lesson course for people aged 13 and older who may feel anxious, powerless, or overwhelmed by AI and media hype. The experience must help learners understand AI, question it, retain their own judgment, and gradually become capable builders.

The emotional movement is:

`uncertainty -> orientation -> agency -> judgment -> creation -> ownership`

The learner—not the technology—is the subject. AI must never feel like a magical authority, surveillance system, companion replacement, or force taking control.

## Premium reference behavior

Study premium yacht, architecture, hospitality, and automotive digital experiences for behavior and craft—not for literal content or visual copying. Useful reference qualities include:

- cinematic, full-bleed visual storytelling;
- a continuous spatial journey rather than a stack of unrelated cards;
- imagery and motion kept central rather than treated as decoration;
- fluid transitions, deliberately oversized moments, and free-form editorial composition;
- tactile materials, calm whitespace, precise typography, and restrained controls;
- seamless movement between interior and exterior states;
- slow ambient motion punctuated by purposeful transformations;
- premium mobile behavior that preserves the experience instead of collapsing into a generic feed.

## Art direction: cinematic oceanic intelligence

Create a distinctive design language inspired by horizon, water, light, navigation, precision engineering, and transformation. It should feel like quiet luxury and crafted editorial motion—not “luxury” as black plus gold.

Explore:

- full-viewport visual chapters with atmospheric moving imagery;
- slow horizon light, refracted water, caustic reflections, fog, translucent depth, and changing focus;
- large editorial typography paired with precise technical labels;
- layered glass planes that move at different depths and reveal context;
- frosted or refractive glass used as an information lens, not as random glassmorphism cards;
- softly curved architectural masks, panoramic crops, and unexpected asymmetry;
- dark, dawn, paper, and high-contrast states that feel like one coherent material system;
- a living progress artwork that begins indistinct and becomes a luminous, self-authored landscape through 50 completed lessons.

Use original or clearly licensed/generated visual assets. Do not use generic robot heads, glowing brains, circuit boards, chat bubbles, neon grids, planets, or stock “AI future” imagery.

## Motion and interaction principles

Motion must help the learner understand location, progress, cause, and consequence.

Build and demonstrate:

1. A cinematic arrival with a restrained image/video reveal and clear immediate action.
2. Smooth route transitions that preserve spatial orientation.
3. Layered glass or image planes that shift subtly with scroll/pointer movement.
4. Masked image reveals and slow Ken Burns-style motion where appropriate.
5. A progress artwork that visibly changes when a lesson is completed.
6. Lesson transitions that feel like moving through a continuous journey, not opening modal cards.
7. Interaction feedback with material response: refracted light, focus, movement, or surface change.
8. Purposeful hover and focus states with equal keyboard clarity.
9. Reduced-motion behavior that preserves all meaning.
10. Mobile motion designed deliberately for touch, performance, and battery—not a shrunken desktop effect.

Avoid constant motion, scroll hijacking, gratuitous 3D, delayed controls, unreadable text over video, cursor gimmicks, and effects that make anxious learners feel watched or out of control.

## Design sequence — do not skip

### Phase 1: audit and principles

First produce:

- a concise diagnosis of V2's strengths;
- a concise diagnosis of the current V3's failures;
- one original brand/product thesis;
- 6-8 testable visual principles;
- 6-8 testable interaction and motion principles;
- explicit anti-patterns that must not appear.

### Phase 2: three original territories

Create three meaningfully different visual territories. Each needs a distinct spatial grammar, material logic, typography system, motion signature, and progress-artwork concept. Do not create three color variations of one layout.

Evaluate each territory against:

- emotional reassurance for an anxious 13+ learner;
- perceived craft and premium quality;
- originality;
- readability and accessibility;
- ability to sustain 50 lessons without repetition;
- motion purpose and performance;
- mobile quality.

Select the strongest territory and explain the decision.

### Phase 3: build the proof

Build the full navigable prototype from the selected territory. Use real lesson names and sample content from the repository. Demonstrate actual transitions and state changes rather than describing them.

## Non-negotiable rejection criteria

Reject and redesign if the result resembles:

- a generic black SaaS admin dashboard;
- a grid of rounded cards with a premium font placed on top;
- black-and-gold luxury clichés;
- a crypto, gaming, space, or sci-fi interface;
- the existing V2 or V3 layout with a new palette;
- a static editorial report rather than a usable learning product;
- a marketing homepage without the complete lesson journey;
- a concept that relies on animation while screenshots remain plain;
- a design whose mobile version becomes a long stack of cards.

## Deliverables

Create:

1. A design-principles and territory-comparison artifact.
2. A full interactive desktop prototype.
3. A full interactive mobile prototype or responsive mode.
4. A motion specification covering triggers, duration ranges, easing, choreography, reduced motion, and performance limits.
5. A component/material system describing typography, color, glass, imagery, grids, controls, focus, and state.
6. A short self-critique listing what still prevents the work from feeling world-class.

Save the new work separately under `PartnerAi/design/` with a clear `claude-design-v3-premium-*` name. Do not overwrite the current V2 or V3. Do not stop after strategy: deliver the working proof.

Make strong creative decisions. Ask only if a missing fact truly blocks the work. The standard is not “clean” or “modern.” The standard is a coherent, cinematic, premium learning journey that makes a nervous learner want to continue and makes progress feel like becoming more capable.
